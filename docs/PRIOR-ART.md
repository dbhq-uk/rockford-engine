## The Rockford Remake Project

## Where it agrees, having been reached a different way

Sahala read the data; we read the code. On every point the two overlap, they agree.

| Claim | Prior art | Here |
|---|---|---|
| `CELLMAPS.BIN` is 40 caves of 40x22, one byte per cell, no header | Confirmed | Confirmed |
| Cave order interleaves shipped and unreleased worlds `1,6,2,7,3,8,4,9,5,10` | From the patch procedure | From the index at `0xFE9D`, `raw world * 4 + stage` |
| Authored `0x20` is the player start, `0x74` is the exit | Confirmed | Loader `0x1093A` and `0x10A02` |
| `0x04` magic wall, `0x08` growing wall, `0x10` amoeba | Confirmed | Loaders `0x10904`, `0x10912`, `0x1092E` |
| `0x2C`-`0x30` explode into treasure, `0x34`-`0x37` do not | "butterflies" and "fireflies" | `0x14322` and the blast kinds |
| `0x78` is a treasure bonus, `0x7C` a time bonus | Confirmed | Loader `0x109E9` onwards |
| The graphics are planar EGA | From M. Eberlein, 2018 | Confirmed |

The enemy row matters most. This repository's cave-census argument concluded that
**both** families yield treasure, and `FORMATS.md` carried that as "confirmed" until
the disassembly overturned it. An independent reading reaching the disassembly's
answer, not the census's, is the strongest check the engine has had.

## Where it is ahead of us: the missing entities have names

We decoded the shape of five authored values from the loader but could only
describe them - "activatable producers", "timed entities". Sahala names them and
describes what they do, from playing the game:

| Authored | Name | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `0x38` | fire tap (magic lamp) | produces fire |
| `0xC4` | acid tap | produces acid |
| `0x0C` | quicksand | lets boulders and treasure fall through at random intervals |
| `0x70` | evil worm | turns treasure into boulders, and boulders into the non-yielding enemy |
| `0x84` | good worm | does the reverse |
| `0x78` | treasure bonus | a random extra amount when collected |
| `0x7C` | time bonus | a random extra amount when collected |

Two further rules, with no counterpart anywhere in our notes: **fire and acid explode
when they touch**, and **treasure dropped onto fire becomes a treasure-yielding
enemy**.

**Both worm rows were confirmed from the code on 17 Sep 2026** and are right in every
particular, including the detail that the two worms are exact opposites. The
disassembly adds only what a player could not see: the boulder-to-enemy conversion is
one time in eight, while the good worm's is every time, and "the non-yielding enemy"
is `0x90`, the anticlockwise family. See "The worms, ported" in
[`PORT-PLAN.md`](PORT-PLAN.md).

The **two bonus rows were confirmed too, and the code explains where the randomness
comes from.** Both are four-frame animations - authored `0x78` loads as `6C` and
cycles `6C`-`6F`, authored `0x7C` loads as `70` and cycles `70`-`73`. The treasure
bonus pays `al - 0x6C` - `0x11709` subtracts that and loops the collect at `0x11713` -
so what you get depends on which frame you happen to touch it on: three treasures,
two, one, or nothing at all. That is the random amount, and it is a reflex test rather
than a dice roll. The time bonus is not random: `0x100D2` adds exactly `0x14`, twenty
seconds, on any of its four frames.

That is the whole of `PORT-PLAN.md` item 14 - the two projectile chains and their
producers, the two timed entities, the rare collectibles - described in plain
English. It also settles the first entry under "things the audit could not settle".

The behaviours still have to be ported from the code, not from this table: prose
does not give thresholds, frame counts or the order the scan applies them in. What
it gives is the target to recognise when the code produces it.

## What it settled here on the day it was found: the EGA palettes

The 204-byte block at unpacked EGA `0x04082` is **12 palettes of 17 bytes**, 16
colour values and a trailing `00`. Entry 0 is `00 01 ... 07 10 ... 17`, the
CGA-compatible default set, which is what identifies the encoding.

Entries 2 to 11 are the ten worlds in raw world order, the same order the caves use:

```
0  menu       4  cook       8  space
1  title      5  player     9  luck
2  hunter     6  cowboy    10  body
3  scuba      7  music     11  miner
```

Two labels differ from the page's. It lists title before menu, where entry 1 is the
one `TITLE.FIL` and `TITLEANI.FIL` both rank first. And it has luck at 5 and player
at 9, where the executable's own filename table at `ds:0x267A` has player at 5 and
luck at 9. Its patcher repository has the pairing the right way round, so the page
is the stale half of that project.

The ordering is the answer to the question `FORMATS.md` had been stuck on. With it,
and with plane order `(0,1,2,3)`, **each of the five shipped worlds picks its own
palette out of the twelve** - 5 out of 5, scored by decoding a world's `.FIL` and its
VGA `.SCM`, mapping each EGA index to the VGA colour it most often coincides with,
and ranking all twelve candidates against that. The earlier failure was not a bad
palette table but a wrong assignment: without the interleave, cook was being coloured
with scuba's palette.

Reading the bytes as IRGB (bit 4 intensity, bits 2-0 red-green-blue) beats the 6-bit
`rgbRGB` reading on four of the five worlds, so IRGB is the better reading, though
the margin is small and the assignment result holds either way.

Sahala found the same table and the same ordering in 2018 and reported that the
sprites still came out wrong. Our check says the assignment is right, so whatever
went wrong there was downstream of it.

This closes the last open item in `FORMATS.md`. It changes nothing about the port,
which plays the VGA release, but it does mean the five never-released worlds now
have both a palette and a name.

## What it checked here: all 160 cave metadata values

The page transcribes the four per-cave tables out of the EGA executable as raw bytes -
treasure quota, score before the quota, score after it, and time limit, 40 words each.
Compared against what `Rockford.Formats.CaveMetadata` reads from the **VGA** image at
`0x245E`, `0x24AE`, `0x24FE` and `0x26A2`: **160 of 160 values identical.**

Two releases, two people, two methods, twenty years apart, no shared working. The
cave metadata is as verified as anything here is going to get.

## What we answered that it left open: which world collects what

The page says plainly: *"No-one knows how the collectibles mentioned in ROCKFORD.EXE
should be connected with the missing themes"*, and guesses four of the ten.

It is in the indexing. The ten treasure names sit at `ds:0x1C81` in fixed 8-byte
fields, and the HUD picks one with `raw world * 8` at `0xFEE7`; the tileset filename
table at `ds:0x267A` is indexed by the same raw world at `0x129DB`. Read the two
together and the pairing falls out with nothing left to guess:

| hunter | scuba | cook | player | cowboy | music | space | luck | body | miner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gold | gems | apples | cups | coins | notes | suns | clubs | hearts | jewels |

Its patcher repository asserts this same pairing without saying how it was reached.
The derivation is in [`FORMATS.md`](FORMATS.md).

## Camoto: the formats, as an editor

Adam Nielsen documented Rockford on ModdingWiki and supported it in Camoto, his
DOS-game modding suite - `fmt-map-rockford.cpp` in libgamemaps, and a game
description in camoto-studio. It is a level editor's view: the formats, not the
rules. There is no engine, no executable analysis and no sound work in it.

It agrees on the shape of everything: `.FIL` and `.CAR` are 320x200 planar EGA, the
level file is 40 caves of 40x22 at one byte per cell.

**It settles the EGA plane order and the colour encoding.** Camoto names the image
format `img-ega-raw-planar-bgri-fullscreen` - planes in the order blue, green, red,
intensity. That is our plane order `(0,1,2,3)` and the IRGB reading of the palette
bytes, both independently, and it closes the one hedge left in `FORMATS.md`, where
IRGB only beat the 6-bit `rgbRGB` reading by a small margin.

**Two things it knows that we did not:**

- **A cell byte is a direct index into the tileset**, which is 20 tiles across. Not a
  separate entity enum with a lookup - the authored byte *is* the tile position.
- **`imageFromCode` has exactly one special case: `if (index == 3) index++`.** Tile 3
  is blank in the sheet, so the ordinary wall is drawn with the next tile along.

Our asset builder does not need either, and is ahead of both: `tools/webassets.py`
reads the game's own sprite tables out of the executable and reproduces the startup
converter at `0x131B3`, including the four-pixel nudges and the bands that are not on
a 16-pixel grid. Camoto's flat grid is the approximation you arrive at without the
executable. ModdingWiki also reports a tile in the hunter sheet drawn at (225,24)
instead of (224,24); that is an EGA-sheet claim and our build uses the VGA sheets, so
it has not been checked.

**Where it is wrong, and it is the same mistake we made.** Camoto assigns levels 1-8
to hunter, 9-16 to cook and so on - five worlds of eight, contiguous. The caves are
ten worlds of four, interleaved, which only the executable's indexing shows. Its
level editor therefore shows twenty caves under the wrong tileset.

**It never found the palettes.** The camoto-studio `TODO` still reads "Figure out
where the Rockford palette is stored". Two projects stuck on the same question, and
it is answered in `FORMATS.md`.

Camoto's list of editable cell values includes codes that appear in no shipped cave -
`0x05`, `0x06`, `0x07`, `0x09`, `0x0A`, `0x0B`, `0x53`, `0x80`, `0x82`. They are
presumably tiles the sheet has art for. Whether the loader accepts any of them is not
checked here; ours falls back to dirt at `0x10A5A` for anything it does not know.

- Its `cellmaps.html`, the 40 caves transcribed to ASCII. We have the file itself, so
  this is only useful as a second opinion on the cell values.
- ~~Offset `0x0B98` in the packed EGA executable, described as a dying on/off toggle,
  with "press R to make coins appear".~~ **Both settled on 25 September 2026, and
  neither holds as stated.** See the debug keyboard section of
  [`FORMATS.md`](FORMATS.md).

  `0x0B98` is not the game's. It is the byte `asahala/Rockford-Patcher` rewrites for
  its own god mode, as a single hex nibble. Patching a copy exactly as that script
  does, unpacking both and diffing them changes one byte of the unpacked image -
  `0x4300`, `0x02` to `0x01` - and nothing reads `ds:0x4300`. It is the middle entry
  of a nine-pair table of row and column offsets, a 3 by 3 neighbourhood in a grid
  two bytes to the cell, and the patch takes that entry off the alignment the other
  eight keep. A hex edit that stops death is not a cheat the game shipped.

  **R is a real key and does something else.** Neither build tests scan code `0x13`
  anywhere; R is matched on its ASCII, at `0x11D7E` in VGA and `0x201A2` in EGA, and
  it sets the end-of-cave byte. It gives up on the cave rather than making coins
  appear. `s` beside it toggles the sound.

  What the page was reaching for does exist, though, and is now documented: a debug
  keyboard on the pause screen, opened by holding Up and Down together with Shift.
  Nine keys in VGA including a cheat toggle, three in EGA with no cheat flag at all.
- The lives counter. The page says the original had limited lives; port item 17 needs
  it for the HUD and it is not located yet.

## Where we are ahead

- `.FIL` / `.CAR` are decoded here; section 3.3 of that page never cracked them, and
  the later note credits the format to a third party rather than to the project.
- The VGA `.SCM` / `.CCM` RLE container and its embedded 32-colour palette are
  decoded here; the page calls VGA a curiosity and gets no extra information out of it.
- The executable is unpacked here. The page hex-edits the packed file at fixed
  offsets, which is why its offsets and ours do not correspond.
- All four per-cave tables are read here from the code with their index arithmetic;
  the page lists quotas, both score tables and times as raw bytes, in the right order,
  without the indexing.
- The music is decoded here - note table, tempo, pattern bank, three order lists, the
  Tandy probe, and a MIDI export. The page says nothing about sound.
- The engine rules come from the disassembly here. The page's rules come from play,
  which is why it has names and we have thresholds.

## Facts it carries that are not in the data

- The hidden levels were for an add-on that was never released. Sahala says this came
  in 2006 from one former Mastertronic employee who had not worked on Rockford, and
  whose own theory it was; he also says that person said the company wanted a true
  sequel instead. **Unverifiable from here, and second-hand.** Others he reports are
  under "What Sahala reports about the hidden worlds' history", below.
- Rockford ran at roughly double the speed of Boulder Dash, and the original had a
  limited number of lives. The lives counter is not yet identified in our disassembly.
- The tilesets hold placeholder player sprites for the missing themes, absent from
  `SPACE.FIL`. **Both halves of that are confirmed here** - ten 16 by 32 figures at
  `y = 144` in every sheet, and the EGA space sheet has two non-zero pixels in that
  band where the others have about 2,490. See `FORMATS.md` and
  `tools/placeholders.py`.

  **The second sentence of this entry used to say they could now be shown "in their
  intended colours" with the palette assignment solved. That was wrong and is
  withdrawn.** Each sheet carries its own coloured copy of the whole cast: between
  two sheets the same figure differs by 41 to 47 per cent of its colour indices
  while its silhouette differs by about 3 per cent. There is no copy of a hidden
  world's figure in that world's own palette, so applying one is a recolour rather
  than a recovery, and the thing the page calls unachieved stays unachieved.

## The Atari ST box: the only photographs of the unreleased worlds

**Found 29 September 2026.** The patcher's README describes "screenshots of these
themes in the back cover of the Atari ST release", and its own copy of the photo is
on imgur, which refuses requests from here. The box itself is scanned at full
resolution on Atari Legend, release 68, as `game_release_scans/82.png` (front) and
`83.png` (back). The back carries eight screenshots, captioned in small type **"SCREEN
SHOTS FROM THE AMIGA VERSION"**. The scans are not copied into this repository.

Read one by one: most are scuba - green seaweed, blue water, yellow diamonds,
seashells - and player - golden cups, picket rows. **One is luck**, the third down on
the left, and it is the only picture of that world anywhere. It shows olive-gold
gravel for the dirt, **black eight-balls** for the boulders, **green four-leaf
clovers** for the treasure, long rows of **playing cards**, a green block band with
red studs, and a cyan haze. Music and miner are not on the box at all.

~~**One reading here differs from the patcher's.**~~ **Withdrawn on 9 October
2026: the cards are worms, as the patcher's README says.** The reading here was
that the screenshot shows the start of an ordinary cave, with the opening
cartoon still up, when each worm is a single egg, so the rows of cards had to be

## What Sahala reports about the hidden worlds' history

From what he told this project in October 2026, paraphrased, and his README as of
commit `79cf5b5`, 8 October 2026. **Reported, not checked
here**, and partly second-hand.

- **Showcase levels.** The Amiga and ST versions had tileset showcase levels,
  which played each theme's introductory animation. He extracted them from the
  Amiga version and ran them in his PC remake. On the PC those animations are
  in the `.CAR` files but never shown; the select screen's title cards
  replaced them. **Checked on 9 October 2026 and half right:** the EGA executables
  never play the first row of the sheet, the `NEW` one, but they do play the other
  three from the game loop (`FORMATS.md`).
- **The 1987 PC release is a proto-version**, with neither the title cards nor
  the showcase levels. He reads `Enter world 1-5` as the remnant of a W-key
  menu dropped when the select screen arrived, which agrees with what
  `FORMATS.md` finds in the code.
- **Why the five were cut: three accounts.** A former Mastertronic employee,
  not involved in Rockford, told him in 2006 the extra worlds were for future
  content, and that the company wanted a true sequel rather than an add-on.
  Simon Plumbe, of the Mastertronic collectors' archive, suggested in 2025 that
  they were cut for quality, and so that the 8-bit and 16-bit releases carried
  the same game when ten worlds and forty caves did not fit on 8-bit media; or
  that they were the original five, replaced; or kept for a developer version.
  Sahala now favours the cut: the 1987 sheets look unfinished to him.
- **An early Amiga developer version** is said in his README to have had some
  of the hidden graphics. No copy is known.
- **No evidence that music and miner were ever drawn**, beyond their player
  sprites. No screenshot survives.
- **The 8-bit Atari and C64 releases carry one hidden-theme level.** His README
  and what he told this project on 11 October: their body level 3 is luck level 4, and their body
  level 4 is the PC's body 3. **The Atari ST disk has this whole set** (see "The
  Atari ST disk"); the 8-bit disks themselves have not been checked, and their
  cave format is not read. The same README lists other differences: hunter 4 is unique, cowboy 4 has
  fewer enemies, and space 1 and 2 and body 1 and 2 come in the other order.
- **The "Amiga box" photo is the PC box.** His `amigaback.png` (1218 by 650, the top
  part) shows a parchment back with "four levels on each of five different worlds",
  doodles of a fish, an octopus, a heart and a parrot, and screenshots. **That is the
  PC (Arcadia / First Star / Mastertronic) box back**, set beside MobyGames' scan of it
  on 12 October: same layout, same doodles, same screenshots. The Amiga box back is
  black, with eight Amiga screenshots and no doodles, and says the same five
  worlds. So the doodles are decoration on a box that shipped five worlds, and the
  idea that scuba art on the Amiga box showed a last-minute change of mind has
  nothing under it. The left screenshot has `GOLD` in its HUD and a pith-helmeted
  player: hunter, not scuba. He made the scuba suggestion on 11 October 2026.
- **The EGA conversion lost colours.** Amiga palettes mapped straight onto the
  fixed EGA palette, so some distinct Amiga colours merged and some worlds have
  fewer than sixteen. The VGA release, with dynamic palettes, is more faithful.
  Michael Eberlein has parsed the VGA graphics files. **Checked on 11 October
  2026, and true:** the PC executable's ten EGA palettes hold only 8 to 13 distinct
  colours in their 16 registers (body 8, cook 9, player, music and luck 10), while
  the Amiga sets for the same worlds hold 28 to 32 distinct colours in 32
  (`tools/amigacols.py cols`). Body and cook lose the most.

His README also dates the 1987 release to December 1987 and says it has been on
archive.org since 2014. `FORMATS.md` has the build as r871109, 9 November 1987,
and the item as uploaded on 13 August 2021.

## Disagreements

- **"20 levels in five difficulty levels"** and 20 hidden. Correct as a count. The
  file holds 40 caves of which 20 are reachable, 4 per world across 10 worlds.
- **The fifth shipped world is called "doctor"** there and `BODY` here. `BODY` is what
  the executable's own strings and filenames say.
- **World 4 and world 8 are swapped** on the page - luck where the executable's
  filename table has player, and player where it has luck. The patcher repository
  has it right, so this is the page being stale rather than the project being wrong.
- **Palette offsets are one byte low** (`0x0929` for a 16-byte title palette). Starting
  one byte earlier gives the clean `00 01 ... 17` default set and a uniform 17-byte
  stride, so the leading `00`, black, is a colour and not a separator.
- ~~**The placeholder order, partly.**~~ **Resolved in its favour on 25 September
  2026.** The page lists the ten as scuba, hunter, cook, cowboy, player, music, space,
  miner, luck, body, and this file briefly disagreed at 4, 7 and 8 - reading a miner,
  a leprechaun and a sportsman where it reads a footballer, a miner and a gambler.
  It is right and that reading was wrong: the studs are the padding of an American
  football helmet and the "beard" is its face guard, and the thing on the next cap is
  a lamp rather than a clover. The patcher repository's README settles it twice over,
  from the Amiga `RF` file's own list of ten worlds and collectibles and from the
  sprite chart at `asahala/rockford/sprites.png`. `FORMATS.md` is corrected.
- **Enemy facing.** The page gives `0x2C` left, `0x2E` up, `0x2F` right, `0x30` down,
  and omits `0x2D`. The loader at `0x10972` treats `0x2C` and `0x2D` as one state and
  the runtime direction order is `0 = left, 1 = up, 2 = right, 3 = down`, held in the
  second runtime byte. See `astra-engine-audit.md`.

## cicodis: the executable, decompiled and running

`gabonator/Projects`, under `CicoJit/gamelib/rockford/`. Gabriel Valky's **cicodis**
is a static recompiler that turns a 16-bit DOS executable into C++; this directory is
Rockford put through it - **10,224 lines of generated C++**, plus an SDL front end, an
EGA emulation header, a patch file, and shell scripts that fetch the game, run the
translation and build the result. It plays.

This is the one that matters most, and it was the last to be found, because nothing
in it mentions Boulder Dash, the level format, or any of the terms the other searches
used. It turned up on a code search for `ROCKFORD.EXE`.

**It is the same binary we disassembled.** `fetch.sh` pulls the 1987 EGA release and
substitutes an unpacked `ROCKFORD.EXE`; the generated `start()` loads 143,040 bytes,
which is our 142,880-byte unpacked EGA image plus its 160-byte header, and enters at
`sub_2d2a5`, which is the EGA entry point `1CFB:02F5` this repository records.

**Addresses map by subtracting `0x10000`.** A `sub_2xxxxx` in that file is our
unpacked **EGA** file offset plus `0x10000`; data offsets appear unchanged.

> releases are separately compiled.

> The sentence went unchallenged because the one thing cicodis was used for -
> the palette blocks - is DATA, and the clause before it says data offsets are
> unchanged. It held for the use it was put to and failed for the use it was
> written to justify. See [`CICODIS-FEASIBILITY.md`](CICODIS-FEASIBILITY.md).

**It settled the palette block boundaries within an hour of being found.** The
selector at `sub_312f4` picks between `0x4071`, `0x4082`, `0x4093` and `0x40A4` and
calls `INT 10h, AX=1002h` with `ES:DX`. That fixes the stride at 17, proves the
trailing `00` is the overscan register, and shows the run starts earlier than the
colour matching here had assumed - the first world sits at `0x40A4`, with a black
fade and two title palettes before it. `FORMATS.md` and `tools/egapal.py` are now
based on that, and the five-of-five world check still passes.

**What it is not.** It is a machine translation, so it is correct by construction and
explains nothing: no names, no structure, `goto` between raw labels, registers as
globals. It answers "what does this code do" only in the sense that you can run it.
Our audit answers "why", which is what a port needs. The two are complements - and
the honest reading is that the hard part of this work was never getting the bytes to
execute, it was working out what they mean.

**The opening it creates.** `PORT-PLAN.md` closes on the admission that "matches"
here means "matches the disassembly as read", never verified against a running game.
A compiled cicodis build is a reference implementation that runs on this machine. It
is the most promising route to the differential replay that item asks for.

**Statically tested on 20 September 2026 -
[`CICODIS-FEASIBILITY.md`](CICODIS-FEASIBILITY.md). Not built and not run**, so
"a running original on this machine" above remains a thing nobody here has
actually done.

The executable cicodis translates is byte-identical to `unpacked/ega.bin` bar
twelve bytes of inert stack padding, so the reference is the same program. But:
the addresses are not comparable with this port's, the build script is
macOS-only, and **it still assumes the translation is faithful - which is now
disproved at one point.** The patched treasure routine zeroes the quota on the
first gem where the original leaves 39. So the assumption in this paragraph was
load-bearing and is not discharged by the binary matching.

"It is the EGA release where our port follows the VGA one" was the line here
carrying the addressing risk, and that turned out to be a finding too.

### The Amiga disks, read on 9 October 2026

Off Sahala's lead that the Amiga had "tileset showcase" levels, and that no copy
with hidden art is known. Both disks are now in `original/amiga/`, and
`tools/amigacols.py` reads them (`ls`, `cat`, `cols`).

- **Two disks, one set of data.** The one above is a crack ("Normal Game /
  Trainer", BET! SOFT Bologna; its game files are `1` and `2`). The other is the
  Melbourne House release itself, archive.org item `Rockford_1988_Melbourne_House`,
  md5 `2de77f3f5ac4ac05da73cea7381df06a`, whose game file is `rf` - the file
  Sahala's README reads. Every data file the two share is byte for byte the same:
  `cols`, `maps`, `imap`, `sel_anis`, `sel_cols`, `title.bin`, `LEV/` and `ANI/`.
  So neither disk has art for a hidden world, and the crack adds nothing.
- **`maps` is the PC `CELLMAPS.BIN`**, md5 `118acb5aff6e7907cc04768cc0fcb128`, all
  forty caves. The Amiga shipped the hidden caves with no art for them, as the
  PC did.
- **`imap` is his showcase level, confirmed in the code** (below). It is 880 bytes,
  one cave's worth, and it is not among the forty in `maps`. Only its top-left 20 by 10 is
  used: a steel border, dirt, boulders, walls and a sample of most other tile
  types. The PC release has no such file. That fits his description, and the code agrees. `ANI/` is read below.
- **`cols` has Amiga palettes for all ten worlds.** 2560 bytes: forty sets of 32
  colours, in the 12-bit Amiga format, four to a world in the cave order of
  `CELLMAPS.BIN`. That order is **confirmed for the five worlds that have art**:
  with sets 0, 8, 16, 24 and 32, the `LEV/` sheets for hunter, cook, cowboy, space
  and doctor come out looking right, and `sel_cols` holds exactly those
  five sets. For the other five it is extrapolated - scuba's four are blue and
  green, and so on - not read from the game's code. It includes **music, luck and
  miner**, whose real colours Sahala's README says are unknown. `render/amiga/cols.png`
  shows all forty. Three colour indexes (2, 4 and 5) carry a stray 0x8000 bit in
  some sets; what it means is not known. Colours only: with no art, this tells us
  what the worlds looked like, not how they were drawn.
- **`LEV/` and `ANI/` are the Amiga's art for the five worlds that have it**, each
  a 320 by 200 screen of five bitplanes, one plane after the other.
  `render/amiga/five-worlds.png` shows them, `ANI/` left and `LEV/` right. `LEV/`
  is the tile and sprite sheet. **`ANI/` is 24 frames of 48 by 48 in a 6 by 4
  grid, and its four rows are labelled `NEW`, `WIN`, `LOSE` and `TIME`** - a
  cartoon for a level starting, being won, being lost and time running out. The PC
  `.CAR` files hold the same sheet, and the EGA game plays rows 2 to 4 and never
  row 1, `NEW` - see `FORMATS.md`, "What plays, and what never does" (#133). Each file is full: 24 of 24 cells used,
  so nothing is hidden in spare space. `ANI/T/` holds four small files (`feet`,
  `eyes`, `diam`, `arms`), not decoded.
- **`sel_anis` is the Amiga select screen's animation, for the five worlds that
  shipped.** 43,200 bytes: 25 frames of 48 by 48, six bitplanes (half-bright
  mode), five frames to a world, with `sel_cols` as the palettes. It was read on
  the evidence of the frame border, which is a solid line and then one bit at each
  edge, and of five background colours that match hunter, cook, cowboy, space and
  doctor. The pictures come out recognisable and not clean, so the frame layout is
  not fully settled. There is nothing in it for a hidden world.
- **The 8-bit ports have no trace of them.** The ZX Spectrum image names hunter,
  cook, cowboy, space and doctor, and no other world. The two C64 images and the
  Atari 8-bit one name none of the ten; they may be packed, so that is weaker.
  The Atari 8-bit credits read "Produced by Icon Design" and "Music by Jas.C.Brooke".
- **Not found:** an Atari ST disk image on archive.org, by search. Not examined:
  the PC floppy `003217-RockfordTheArcadeGame`, and anything off archive.org.

### The Amiga game file, decompiled on 11 October 2026

The Melbourne House disk's game is `rf`, one 26,332-byte block of 68000 code and
data with 543 relocations. `tools/amigahunk.py` reads it and, with Capstone
installed (`pip install capstone`), disassembles it. Addresses below are offsets
into that block. Nothing here was run: it is read from the disassembly.

- **It loads any of ten worlds by number.** The routine at `0x0CD6` takes the world
  number (`$6616`), multiplies it by 9 and indexes a name block at `0x5116`, which
  holds `XXX/hunt`, `lev/scub`, `lev/cook`, `lev/play`, `lev/cowb`, `lev/cond`,
  `lev/astr`, `lev/gamb`, `lev/doct` and `lev/mine` at a 9-byte stride: the raw-world
  order of the PC, with the Amiga's names (conductor, astronaut, gambler). It opens
  `lev/<world>` and `ani/<world>` in turn by overwriting the first three letters,
  and reads 40,000 bytes of each. Then it reads **256 bytes of `cols` at the world
  number times `0x100`**, and **3,520 bytes of `maps` at the world number times
  `0xDC0`**, which is four caves of 880. That settles the palette layout from code,
  not from looks: four 32-colour palettes a world, for all ten worlds.
- **A missing world file hangs the game.** Every failed open jumps to `0x4D62`,
  which flips the screen's background colour between yellow and grey for ever.
  The code has no path that skips a world whose files are not there.
- **The menu reaches five worlds.** It maps its choice to a raw world through a
  five-word table at `0x55D2`: `0, 2, 4, 6, 8`. The PC's executable has ten words
  there, `0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 1, 3, 5, 7, 9`, and so a hidden-world path that the Amiga
  game lacks. On the Amiga the hidden worlds cannot be chosen at all.
- **Every world has a fifth level, and it is `imap`.** The routine at `0x3268`
  copies a cave into the working buffer. If the cave number is 5 it copies the
  `imap` level, and otherwise cave 0 to 3 of the loaded world, 880 bytes each.
  That is Aleksi Sahala's tileset showcase level, now confirmed in the code, and it
  is one level for every world. `imap` is loaded at start-up with the title and
  select-screen files (the table at `0x0B70`).
- **The world names are there for all ten**, as `The HUNTER`, `The SCUBA`,
  `The COOK`, `The PLAYER`, `The COWBOY`, `The CONDUCTOR`, `The ASTRONAUT`,
  `The GAMBLER`, `The DOCTOR` and `The MINER`, at `0x55F8` on a 72-byte stride, used
  for a world's title text.

What this says about the hidden worlds on the Amiga: the code, the palettes and the
caves all handle ten worlds, and what is missing is five pairs of files and the
menu entries. That fits an unfinished or cut set, and it fits Aleksi's account of an
early build that had some of the files. It does not show whether the files ever
existed.

### Rocky Clone: the Amiga programmer's public-domain game plays Rockford's forty caves, 12 October 2026

Found through a YouTube comment (below). **Verified here, by unpacking it:**

- **Aminet `game/misc/Rocky.lha`**, 48,810 bytes, "Boulderdash !. V1.0",
  author Laurence Vanhelsuwé, dated 1989.09.02, "Source: Fish collection". Fred
  Fish's own note says it "was submitted to me on disk directly by the author", 22
  March 1992. Contents: the `Rocky` executable, **`Rocky.s` (3,187 lines of 68000
  assembly)**, `maps`, `chars` and its source `chars.iff`, build files and a
  ReadMe. Unpacked with a pure-Python LHA reader, in an isolated folder.
- **`Rocky\maps` is byte for byte Rockford's `CELLMAPS.BIN`**: 35,200 bytes, md5
  `118acb5aff6e7907cc04768cc0fcb128`. The ReadMe: "There are 40 problem-packed
  (solvable) levels in all." So the game plays all forty, the twenty hidden ones
  included, one year after Rockford, in a program with its own 8 by 8 graphics (its
  `chars` are three bitplanes of 320 by 200, nothing to do with Rockford's art).
- **`Rocky.s` names the cell codes.** `AIR $00`, `GRASS $01`, `RWALL $02`, `WALL
  $03`, `EWALL $04` (magic wall: `MAGIC_TIME` 350 generations), `AMIBA $10`, `MAN
  $20`, `ROCK $28`, `FROCK $2C`, `BUTTER_FLY $30`, `FIRE_FLY $34`, `FIRE_TAP $38`,
  `BEGG $70` and `GEGG $84` (eggs, hatching in 200 and 100 generations), `EXIT
  $74`, `NUMBER $78`, `EXTRA_T $7C` (`BONUS_SECS` 20), **`EXTRA_L $80`**, `DIAMOND
  $88`, green and blue snakes at `$90` and `$D0`, `WATER_TAP $C4`. They agree with
  `FORMATS.md` wherever both speak; the new one is `EXTRA_L`, an extra-life cell. It
  occurs in none of the forty caves, and it **does** occur in the Amiga's showcase
  level `imap` (cell 17, 8), which is the one place a cell no shipped cave uses is
  in play.
- **Its comments name creatures for the two kinds of mover.** The `BUTFLY` cells
  (`$30`, anticlockwise) are "bat, pirhanas, birds" and the `FIREFLY` cells (`$34`,
  clockwise) are "monkeys, fish". That is the Amiga programmer's own gloss, in 1989.
  It lists creatures and does not say which world each belongs to, so "piranhas and
  fish" is a hint for scuba and "bats" and "monkeys" for hunter, and no more than a
  hint.
- **It is a 1989 cellular-automaton reading of the same rules**, written as "a
  version of LIFE" with "over 100 states", independently of the PC port. A reference
  for anything the PC disassembly leaves unclear, not a source for the Rockford code:
  Rocky is a clone with its own engine and its own time and drop constants.

### The two arcade photographs against the builds' tiles, 12 October 2026 (#154)

Done by eye, the photographs opened as page images beside the faithful sheet
(`music.png`, `scuba.png`) and VIP's atlas (`music-x8.png`, `scuba-x8.png`), tile by

- **Agrees.** Shells are the boulders. The dirt is a speckled block. The gems are
  pointed.
- **Differs, and by more than in music.** The shell is **grey-white** in the

  and the sheet's are a multicoloured coral mosaic. The gem is **yellow and green**,
  and the sheet's is red and yellow. Rockford is a **red octopus with large white
  eyes** carrying a round disc, and the sheet draws a **diver in a helmet**. The

**What it means** (my reading, not established). Arcadia's board was an Amiga
(Zzap!64, page 70: "based around the Commodore Amiga board"; the C64 box:

differently. The photographs show the **shapes agreeing and the colours and the player not**. That is

octopus, so the player may be a different design and not only a different palette.

the arcade, the cheap and clear wins are, in scuba, the shell, dirt, wall and gem
colours, and in music the treasure colour; each is a redraw and Dan's call.

  skeletons, "the ears are flying around", eyes, skulls, graves).
- **It confirms Rocky's creature gloss for hunter**: bats and monkeys are the two
  mover types there, as `Rocky.s` says. And that "snakes have magical powers", some
  turning rock into treasure and others treasure into rock, "walls will grow
  horizontally", "clocks gain you extra time", and that in the Doctor's world "the
  taps drip blood": the cells `FORMATS.md` names.
- **PC keys** (R restarts, Q quits, B changes background colour, F1 help, S toggles
  sound) and the Atari ST's F10 restart. Nothing on the debug keyboard.

### The other copies, checked 12 October 2026

Every file below was compared by MD5 against what this repository already holds,
with this repository's own readers; a research pass found them, and its own parser
was not trusted.

- **Amiga, TOSEC `commodore-amiga-games-adf-r`: 20 Rockford ADFs**, all downloaded and
  read here. The plain one is **byte for byte our Melbourne House ADF** and the
  `[cr Champs][t Lightforce]` one is **byte for byte our BET! SOFT crack** (both by
  MD5). **The other 16 cracks and hacks keep every data file identical to ours**
  (`cols`, `maps`, `LEV/`, `ANI/`, `imap`, titles); what differs is
  `s/startup-sequence`, `.fastdir` and, in six, the loader `rf`. None has a file named
  for scuba, player, conductor, gambler or miner. **The last two, `Rockford II` and
  `Rockford III` `[cr][p No One Inc]`, are fan-made hacks**: 36 of the 40 caves in
  `maps` are changed, the tile and cartoon sheets are repainted, and one has a
  "Rockford.REMIX" file. Not the original's data, and no hidden-world art either.
- **Amiga WHDLoad**: `whdload.de` `Rockford.lha` is a 4,587-byte installer with no
  game data; the Internet Archive's `Rockford_v1.0.zip` (272,078 bytes) answered HTTP
  500 on every try here, but the research pass read it as a WHDLoad folder with
  `ANI/` and `LEV/` for the five worlds only. No IPF, SPS/CAPS, Kryoflux or cover-disk
  entry was found anywhere.
- **Atari ST**: the `.st` we hold is TOSEC's **[ce Exceptions] crack**. TOSEC also has
  an **original Pasti dump** (`.stx`, 424,208 bytes, md5
  `de31e33d4b8635c3c58ceba53edf6cb6`), and Atarimania has a flux dump (HFE, STX, SCP
  and HxC streams, 46 MB) that was not downloaded. **The original holds the same
  caves as the crack**: all 20 of the ST's caves are in it (17 whole, the rest split
  across sector boundaries), **luck 4 among them**. So the luck cave is in the
  released game and not only in a cracked copy. Four compilation menu disks carry a
  packed copy and were not compared. Atarimania also lists a US "Arcadia Software,
  1989" ST release (Electronic Arts distribution) with no dump.
- **PC**: four more Internet Archive items - the digitoxin 1987 `[!]` pair, goodolddays
  `000342`, the ibmpc5150 5.25" 1988 disks and the loose-file 1987 install - **hold the
  same files as ours**, byte for byte, apart from a 256-byte `SLOW.COM` and, on the
  5.25" disk 2, the second `ROCKFORD.EXE` build described above. The digitoxin pair is
  an independent confirmation of our r871109 copy.
- **Arcade**: still not dumped. arcade-history.com lists it as "(c) 1987 Arcadia
  Systems", on the Super Select board, with "nine scenarios with five levels each",
  a count the home versions do not share.
- **CPC**: CPC-Power also lists a Mastertronic compilation, "Rockford + Back to
  Reality", distributed in Spain by Dro Soft. No dump format was shown for it.

### What the Amiga game file says about all ten worlds, 12 October 2026

The 72-byte world records in `rf` that carry the names (`The HUNTER` ... `The MINER`)
also carry, for **all ten worlds, the Amiga's own treasure quota and time limit for
each of the four caves**, at `+0x38` and `+0x40` of each record, read by the routine
at `0x2820`. `tools/amigaworlds.py` prints them against the PC's; `--selftest` pins
values read by hand. Nothing here was run.

- **The five worlds the Amiga shipped differ from the PC in a few caves.** Quota:
  hunter 4 is 100 (PC 150), cowboy 4 is 35 (PC 55), doctor 1 is 12 (PC 18). Time:
  cowboy 2 and 4 are 145 and 140 (PC 115, 115), doctor 2 is 180 (PC 160), and doctor
  3 is **65 (PC 170)**. Cook, space and scuba match the PC exactly.
- **The four hidden worlds the PC tuned later look unfinished on the Amiga.**
  Music: quota 50, 30, 30, 30 and times 130, 90, 110, 140 (PC 50, 30, 30, 40 and
  130, 90, 110, 110). Luck: quota 20, 8, 40, 20 and times 140, 140, 140, 160 (PC 15,
  11, 30, 65 and 130, 130, 140, 145). Miner: quota 18, 20, 60, 30 and **time 140 in
  all four caves** (PC 16, 24, 57, **1** and 140, 140, 110, 180). Player: quota 16,
  35, 30, 30 (PC 20, 35, 30, 33). A time of 140 four times over, and the unidentified
  longword at `+0x18` being 45 in all of the last five worlds' caves, read as
  defaults that nobody had tuned. **That is an inference**, not something the file
  says.
- **So the Amiga's hidden-world quotas and times are not the PC's, and the PC's are
  the tuned ones.** Sahala's README takes the PC's as "100% accurate" for the hidden
  worlds; they are the PC's, and the Amiga's earlier values are a second source for
  how the design moved. The caves themselves are the same: the Amiga's `maps` is
  byte for byte the PC's `CELLMAPS.BIN`. The PC's miner 4, a quota of 1, is the
  single-jewel cave Sahala describes, and the Amiga gave it 30.
- **The Amstrad CPC disk** agrees with the Amiga on hunter 4 (100) and not on cowboy
  4 (CPC 60, Amiga 35, PC 55), so it is neither the Amiga's set nor the PC's.

### The goodolddays 5.25" disks, 12 October 2026

Archive.org item `003217-RockfordTheArcadeGame` (goodolddays.net, labelled "1988,
Unverified"): two 360K disks, now in `original/vga-525/`. They are the **VGA release
on 5.25" floppies**: `.SCM` and `.CCM`, not `.FIL` and `.CAR`. Every data file is
byte for byte our VGA release's; **`ROCKFORD.EXE` is a different build** (31,805
bytes, CRC32 `6558ca05`, against 31,789 and `4503fd98`). Unpacked, the data segment
is identical, and the code differs in three small edits - 2 bytes inserted at
`0xEA18`, 1 at `0xFB0C`, 3 deleted at `0x13C4D` - plus four identical video-register
constants changed from 5 to 1 (`0xEF63` and three like it), with every call and jump
after them shifted to match. All in the display code, none in a table or a rule.
Neither executable is in tdc-database, so this has no release number. It adds no
world, no cave and no art.

### Smaller checks, 12 October 2026

- **The `0x8000` bit** on colours 2, 4 and 5 of some `cols` palettes: a search of

### The C64 and Atari 8-bit caves, attempted 12 October 2026 and not found

Aleksi Sahala says these two releases use luck 4 as body 3. The Atari ST original
already shows that, so this tries to see it in the 8-bit games themselves. **It did
not get there**, and what was learned is recorded so it is not repeated.

- **The two C64 images (`[h Ikari]` and `[cr Sharks]`) are cracks, each with four
  layers.** One program, `R'FORD++`, with an Exomizer-style decruncher at `$0334` that
  unpacks the program three times over: the intro and a key press, then a second
  unpack, then a third, which ends in the crackers' **trainer menu** at `$9000`
  ("TRAINED BY JUST ICE OF IKARI", infinite lives and time, "SORRY BUT ANOTHER
  BOULDERDASH GAME!!"), and only after that the game. No world name appears in the
  clear at any layer, and the quota table is not plain bytes, words or BCD in any of
  them. The game's credit line says "M.A.D. presents, produced by Icon Design, music
  by Jas. C. Brooke".
- **What worked:** a CPU-only 6502 emulation (py65) runs the first two layers and the
  third to the trainer, once the KERNAL calls are stubbed and the stubs are put back
  after each layer, which overwrites the top of memory. VICE 3.7 with the open-source
  OpenROMs also reached the second layer and then jammed in the third, because the
  stage depends on the BASIC pointers in zero page that OpenROMs sets differently
  from Commodore's ROM.
- **What would finish it:** unpacking the fourth layer, which is behind the trainer
  menu and was not reached. A different, un-cracked C64 copy (the original M.A.D.
  tape) would skip the crack entirely; none was found (`PRIOR-ART.md`, "The other
  copies"). The Atari 8-bit image has a custom boot loader and is packed the same
  way. Neither was read.
- **Not worth more on its own:** it would show how the 8-bit caves differ, and the ST
  original and the manual already give the answer that matters.

### The Amstrad CPC disk, read on 12 October 2026

Dan downloaded it from CPC-Power (sheet 1823); it is in `original/amstrad-cpc/`.
It is the **cracked "Trainer" disk** (ACS, November 1988), md5
`bf651a62420cf597b4777082e5551b30`, not the clean tape. A standard extended
`.DSK`, 40 tracks, CP/M directory: a BASIC loader, `ROCKFOR1.BIN` (the game, 15 KB)
and `ROCKFOR2.BIN` to `ROCKFOR6.BIN` (11 KB each, one per world). Read with
throwaway scripts; nothing is in `tools/` yet.

- **Five worlds, no hidden ones.** `ROCKFOR1.BIN` names `THE HUNTER`, `THE COOK`,
  `THE COWBOY`, `THE ASTRONAUT` and `THE DOCTOR`, and no other. As on the Spectrum
  image, nothing here names a hidden world.
- **The caves are not stored the way the PC stores them**, and the world files
  hold CPC screen graphics, so the layouts are not compared. The cave format is
  not decoded.
- **The per-level treasure quotas are readable**: twenty three-digit numbers
  after the world names. Against the PC's, for the same five worlds in the same
  order, **14 of 20 match and six differ**: hunter 4 (100, PC 150), cowboy 4 (60,
  PC 55), space 1 (50, PC 26) and doctor 1, 2 and 4 (60, 16, 50; PC 18, 70, 18).
  Hunter 4 and cowboy 4 are two of the changes Aleksi Sahala's README gives for
  the 8-bit releases. The rest of his list does not show up here: doctor 3 is 75
  as on the PC, not luck 4's 65. So the CPC has changes of its own, and it is
  not the ST's cave set. The alignment of the twenty numbers was read by eye and
  is not proved.
- **Nothing here changes what is known about the hidden worlds.**

### The Atari ST disk, read on 11 October 2026

An ST disk image is on the Internet Archive, in the "Playtime Atari ST"
collection as `Rockford (Europe).zip`. It is now in `original/atari-st/`, md5
`cf4ae2a4b55c9493c7495d42068e9ebd`, 377,856 bytes, and `tools/stcaves.py` reads
it. Whether it is a clean dump has not been checked: Atarimania lists a dump in
preservation formats (HFE, Pasti, RAW, SCP), and Emu-Land a TOSEC-named copy, and
neither has been compared with this one. It was found in a desk search of the
archives on 11 October 2026.

- **What is on it.** A plain FAT12 disk: `AUTO/ROCK.PRG` and `AUTO/FORD.PRG`
  (68000 programs) and `DATA.D`, 294,316 bytes, which is a 2,360-byte loader
  followed by the game's assets. `FORD.PRG` names `huntera.pi`, `cooka.pi`,
  `cowboya.pi`, `spacea.pi`, `doctora.pi`, `fred.pi`, `maps.m`, `cooksps.s` and
  `huntsps.s`. No hidden world is named.
- **Its graphics** are in the ST's own four-bitplane low-resolution format and
  decode on sight: the title, tile sheets, and animation sheets with `NEW`, `WIN`,
  `LOSE` and `TIME` labelled, as on the Amiga. All that was seen is for the five
  worlds that shipped. About 260 KB of it has not been decoded one picture at a
  time, so "nothing for a hidden world" is what was seen, not a proof.
- **Its caves are the last 20 x 880 bytes of `DATA.D`**, in the same cell format
  as `CELLMAPS.BIN`, and mostly the same caves. In the ST's order:

  | ST cave | What it is, against the PC's 40 |
  |---|---|
  | hunter 1 to 4 | hunter 1 (98%, altered), hunter 2, **hunter 4**, and a **fourth that matches no PC cave** (best 71%) |
  | cook 1 to 4 | cook 1, 2, 3, and cook 4 (95%, altered) |
  | cowboy 1 to 4 | cowboy 1 (99.9%), cowboy 2 (78%, mostly rebuilt), cowboy 3, cowboy 4 (98%, altered) |
  | space 1 to 4 | **space 2, space 1**, space 3, space 4 |
  | body 1 to 4 | **body 2, body 1**, **luck 4**, **body 3** |

  So **a luck cave was released**: the ST's third body cave is the PC's luck 4,
  byte for byte, and the ST's fourth body cave is the PC's body 3.
- **This is Aleksi Sahala's list, and it is on the ST.** His README gives these
  differences as being in the 8-bit Atari and C64 releases, and "not present in
  the Amiga, Atari ST and the PC releases": hunter 4 unique, cowboy 4 modified,
  space 1 and 2 swapped, body 1 and 2 swapped, body 3 replaced by luck 4, body 4
  the PC's body 3. Every item is on this ST disk. Either his README is wrong
  about the ST, or the 8-bit releases were built from the ST's cave set. The 8-bit
  disks themselves have not been checked; their cave format is not decoded here.

### Rockford-Outskirts, in detail

Kept because the giveaways generalise, and because the README is convincing at a
glance - it is the only other 2026-dated "Rockford reverse engineering" result on
GitHub, and it is dressed as preservation work.

**The repository.** Created 25 August 2026 at 10:22 and finished at 10:24. Six
commits, two minutes, messages generated from a word list: "bug sync #1402",
"style parse #191". Fifty-five stars on a repository with no code. The whole tree is
a README, an `index.html`, two SVGs, a workflow, and a ten-byte file with a random
name.

**The workflow.** `.github/workflows/*.yml`, commented `# keep repo live`, runs
hourly, sleeps a random number of minutes, then commits a random string to that
ten-byte file with a randomly assembled message. Its only purpose is to keep the
repository looking maintained in search results and trending lists.

**The README.** Fluent nonsense: "procedural pixel inference", a
"constraint-satisfaction solver" for level descriptors, "17 language translations"
including Swahili, SID chip resynthesis for a game that has no SID chip, and the
"lost 30" chambers - a number that is simply wrong, since there are 20 hidden caves
and that is checkable from the file size. Build instructions for a CMake project
that does not exist in the repository.

**The payload route.** The download badge points at the project's GitHub Pages site.
That page is an obfuscated loader: ten hidden `div`s of base64, XORed against a key,
decoded, written over the document, and any embedded scripts executed. Decoded
statically, it is a counterfeit GitHub release page - "release-x64.zip, 122.2 MB,
SHA-256 verified, SLSA level 3, signed provenance, scanned by ClamAV and five
engines" - and the actual link goes to `unlocktool.click`. Every reassurance on that
page is text on a page that anyone can write.

**Reported to GitHub as malware distribution on 15 September 2026** (Dan). If the
repository is gone by the time you read this, that is why.

**What generalises.** A repository created in minutes with no source, a workflow whose
job is to fake activity, star counts that do not match the content, a download that
leaves GitHub for a domain with no history, and security claims written as prose
rather than shown as artefacts. The archive was not fetched, and nothing from it has
been run.
